Mile Marker

Mile Marker #3: Managing My Time & Energy

The assignment, with support, and a form you can fill in and save.

About 25 minutes 9 of 10 in this module

You have the same 168 hours as everyone else, but you don't have the same brain, the same responsibilities, or the same energy reserves.

This Mile Marker isn't about building the perfect schedule. It's about starting to understand how you actually work: your patterns, your energy, and your limits. You'll come back to what you learn here when you build your capstone project.

What you'll do

  1. Before you start, finish these two Trail Tools. You'll use them in Questions 1 and 3.
    • Where My Time Goes (your time log)
    • My Week at a Glance (your week plan)
  2. Answer four questions in the form below.
  3. Save as PDF and upload it to the Mile Marker #3 assignment in Blackboard.

●○AI use: AI-supported

You may use AI as a thinking partner to explore your ideas. Work with what it gives you, then write your responses in your own voice. See the AI Course Policy in the syllabus and Lesson 1.4: AI use labels.

Optional prompt to try: "Ask me questions to help me understand how my energy and focus work throughout the day, and what that means for how I should structure my time." Use what's useful. Your final responses must be in your own words.

Your Mile Marker

Type your answers in the boxes. Your typing is saved in this browser as you go, so you can come back later on the same device. Nothing is sent anywhere until you upload it.

Mile Marker #3: Managing My Time & Energy

Enter your name before saving.

Question 1Your Patterns

Look at your Where My Time Goes log.

A strong answer

Uses your log to name one real pattern or time-waster, explains why it happens, and says what it costs you. Not a general statement that could apply to anyone.

Look back at: Lesson 3.3: Find your best hour

Question 2Your Energy

Think about the five batteries: physical, mental, emotional, social, and spiritual.

A strong answer

Names one battery, explains what's draining it in your life right now, and describes one small action that's realistic for this week.

Look back at: Lesson 3.5: The five batteries

Question 3Your Week

Look at the week you mapped in My Week at a Glance.

A strong answer

Names your real non-negotiables, explains why your most important work landed where your energy is best, and connects one part of the plan to something you value.

Look back at: Lesson 3.2: Map your week in three steps

Question 4Your Self-Knowledge

This one doesn't have a wrong answer.

To get you thinking (no separate answer needed)
  • Did anything in this module describe how your brain works in a way you hadn't had words for before? You don't have to name a diagnosis.
  • Why is your habit realistic for your life right now?
A strong answer

Names one specific thing you're learning about yourself and why it matters, and describes one small, realistic habit with a clear way to tell whether it's working. Honest beats polished.

Look back at: Lesson 3.6: Strategies that work for more brains

In the print window, choose Save as PDF (or Microsoft Print to PDF) as the printer. The file name will start with "Mile Marker #3".

Submit it

Upload your PDF to the Mile Marker #3 assignment in Blackboard. Check Blackboard for the due date.

Prefer paper? A printable handout is with the assignment in Blackboard. Fill it in by hand, then upload a photo or scan.

Optional: attach your time log or week plan too.

How it's scored (4 points): full points means every prompt is answered, and each answer is about you, with at least one real detail. See How Mile Markers are scored.
