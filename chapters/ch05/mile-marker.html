Mile Marker #5: Understanding My Money

The assignment, with support, and a form you can fill in and save.

About 20 minutes 7 of 8 in this module

The point is to know yourself a little better when it comes to money. Nobody needs a perfect budget.

Leave this module with one honest insight and one realistic intention. There are no right or wrong financial situations here, and you never need to share a dollar amount.

What you'll do

  1. Before you start, finish these two Trail Tools. You never have to share them.
    • Dollar Tracker (your spending, for Question 1)
    • Budget Builder (your zero-based budget, for Question 4)
  2. Answer five questions in the form below.
  3. Save as PDF and upload it to the Mile Marker #5 assignment in Blackboard.

●○AI use: AI-supported

You may use AI as a thinking partner to explore your ideas. Work with what it gives you, then write your responses in your own voice. Never share real account numbers, income, debt, or other personal financial details with an AI tool. Use general descriptions only: "I spend a lot on food," not "I spend $347 a month on groceries." See the AI Course Policy in the syllabus and Lesson 1.4: AI use labels.

Optional prompt to try: "Ask me questions to help me understand my spending patterns and identify one realistic change I could make, without sharing any personal financial details." Use what's useful. Your final responses must be in your own words.

Your Mile Marker

Type your answers in the boxes. Your typing is saved in this browser as you go, so you can come back later on the same device. Nothing is sent anywhere until you upload it.

Mile Marker #5: Understanding My Money

Enter your name before saving.

Question 1What You Noticed

Look at your Dollar Tracker.

To get you thinking (no separate answer needed)
  • What surprised you, or confirmed something you already suspected?
  • Which category took more than you expected? Where did small purchases add up?
A strong answer

Names one real pattern from your tracker, like a category or a kind of small purchase, and says what it shows about your actual priorities.

Look back at: Dollar Tracker; Lesson 5.3: Three steps to seeing clearly

Question 2Your Financial Reality

Describe your situation honestly, not for judgment but for clarity. No numbers needed.

A strong answer

Describes your situation honestly without numbers, names your biggest pressure and one support, and explains financial wellness in your own words, connected to your schoolwork or another part of your wellness.

Look back at: Lesson 5.2: The eight dimensions of wellness; Money help at OCTC

Question 3One Realistic Next Step

It doesn't have to be dramatic. It just has to be honest and doable for your life right now.

A strong answer

Names one specific step, connects it to something you noticed, and explains why it's realistic for your life right now.

Look back at: Lesson 5.3: Find your money leaks

Question 4Your Zero-Based Budget

Look at the budget you built in the Budget Builder. No dollar amounts needed.

A strong answer

Explains why you chose your categories, says whether your budget balances to zero (and what you'll adjust if it doesn't), and names one thing building it showed you.

Look back at: Budget Builder; Lesson 5.3: Give every dollar a job

Question 5Keeping It Private

If you didn't use AI for this module, that's a complete answer. Just say so.

A strong answer

Names what you kept out of your prompts and how you worded things instead, or says plainly that you didn't use AI.

Look back at: Lesson 5.3: AI as a money thinking partner

In the print window, choose Save as PDF (or Microsoft Print to PDF) as the printer. The file name will start with "Mile Marker #5".

Submit it

Upload your PDF to the Mile Marker #5 assignment in Blackboard. Check Blackboard for the due date.

In an in-person class? Your instructor may give you a printed copy. Fill it in by hand, then upload a photo or scan.

Optional: attach your tracker or budget too. That's your choice.

How it's scored: full credit means every prompt is answered, and each answer is about you, with at least one real detail. See How Mile Markers are scored.
