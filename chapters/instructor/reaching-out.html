Reaching Out: Checkpoints and Early Alerts

The 12-week checkpoint calendar, Starfish flags and kudos, and how to write to the student.

About 10 minutes 3 of 23 in this guide
Pending review

This part is a draft under review. It isn't course policy yet, so use it as guidance until the review is complete.

Most students who fall behind in FYE 100 don't need a lecture. They need someone to notice early, say so kindly, and point to a next step.

OCTC doesn't run required Starfish progress surveys, so this part is the shared plan: when to look, what to send, and how to use Starfish flags and kudos so they read as support, not a pile-on.

What every section does

  1. Let Blackboard tell you. The master shell's student performance alerts flag a student after 2 missed assignments or 7 days since their last login. Keep those settings in your section, and check your alerts each week.
  2. Reach out first. When an alert comes in, send the student a short, private message by whatever channel works for you: email, Blackboard Messages, or something else. Name what's missing, say it can still be made up in the catch-up window, and ask what would help.
  3. Use Starfish when you need more than yourself. Raise a flag if the student doesn't respond, or if what's going on is bigger than this course. Flags go to the student and their Success Coach, so the student gets a second person in their corner. You don't need to know the details to raise one.
  4. Notice the good, too. Send kudos when a student shows up, catches up, or does something well. It takes a minute and it's one of the most effective things in Starfish.

Recommended 12-week checkpoint calendar

Recommended A shared starting point, not a requirement. Adjust it to your section.

Getting into Starfish

  1. Open Starfish from MyPath. Scroll to Discover More at the bottom of the MyPath page and select Starfish.
  2. Find the student. Use the search box in the upper right corner.
  3. Pick an action from the toolbar on the student's page. Flag and Kudos are the two you'll use most.

Raising a flag

A flag goes to the student and their Success Coach. Choose the one that names the real problem:

Which Starfish flag to raise
FlagRaise it when
Not Yet Logged Into BlackboardA student hasn't accessed the course. In FYE 100, by Thursday of Week 1.
Attendance ConcernIn-person sections: missed classes are starting to affect the grade.
Missing / Late Assignment(s)Work is missing and the student hasn't answered your message.
Warning: Course Success ConcernMissing work or low scores put passing at risk.
Recommend Drop / Recommend WithdrawalOnly after you've talked with the student. Dropping or withdrawing can affect financial aid, so the Success Coach needs to be part of that conversation.

One flag at a time

A student who opens their email to several flags at once can read it as hostile, even when every one is accurate. Raise the highest-ranking flag: the concern that explains the others.

  • Not logging in comes first. If a student hasn't logged in for ten days, that's the flag, not each assignment they've missed since.
  • A pattern beats a single item. If passing is at risk, raise Warning: Course Success Concern instead of a Missing / Late flag for each assignment.
  • Send your own message first when you can, so the flag isn't the first they hear from you. The exception is a student you can't reach, like one who hasn't logged in by Thursday of Week 1.
  • Close the loop. When the student re-engages, clear the flag, and consider a kudos.

Kudos: the underused one

Kudos may be the most effective thing you can send in Starfish. For many first-year students, it's the first time someone at the college has noticed them doing something right. It costs you a minute and it changes how the next flag, if there ever is one, will land.

  • Off to a great start! First Mile Marker in, on time.
  • Keep up the good work! Steady, specific work over several weeks.
  • Showing Improvement! A student who used the catch-up window, or turned a general answer into a specific one.
  • Don't Give Up A student who's struggling but still showing up.
  • Great Job! Anything worth naming.

A good habit: aim for at least one kudos for every student by the end of the semester. The checkpoint calendar suggests when.

Writing the comment

Every flag and kudos has a comment box, and the student reads it. Write it to the student, the way you'd talk to them after class. It isn't a report to the Success Coach about the student, and it isn't an incident report.

Don't

"Student has not submitted Mile Markers 3 and 4 and has not responded to emails. Please follow up."

Written about the student, to someone else.

Don't

"Failure to submit required assignments. Current grade: 58%."

Sterile, like an incident report. Nothing to do next.

Do

"Hi Jordan, I've missed seeing your work the last two weeks. Mile Markers 3 and 4 are still open until the catch-up date, and they can still earn full credit. Your Success Coach is getting this too, so you have two people in your corner. Reply to whichever of us is easier."

To the student, specific, and with a next step.

Do (kudos)

"Your Mile Marker #2 was so specific. Naming who you'd ask and exactly what you'd say is what this course is about. Keep it up."

Names what they did, so they can do it again.

A quick test: would you be comfortable if the student read it aloud to you? If not, rewrite it.

A first message you can send

For your own outreach, before any flag:

Hi [name], I noticed [Mile Marker #3 and the Module 3 discussion] haven't come in yet. You can still submit them until [the catch-up date], and they're graded the same as on-time work. Is anything getting in the way? If you tell me what would help, we can make a plan.

Once you're in contact, Coaching a student who falls behind in Grading and Late Work covers what to say next.
