Module 9: Think It Through — Critical Thinking + STAR(T) Introduction

Slides, coaching notes, active learning ideas, and an in-class discussion.

Slides, coaching notes, and in-class ideas for Module 9: Think It Through — Critical Thinking + STAR(T) Introduction.

At a glance

  • I can identify one thinking habit that sometimes gets in my way and name a strategy for interrupting it.
  • I can apply the 4C Check (Clarity, Context, Credibility, Consequences) to evaluate a real situation or decision.
  • I can explain the STAR(T) framework and describe how each of the five steps works.
  • I can draft one practice STAR(T) response using a real experience and apply the 4C Check to strengthen it.

Main assignment Mile Marker #9: Think It Through

Slides

Title, a Breaking Camp housekeeping slide for after spring break, the Skill Spotlight, thought traps, the 4C Check, STAR(T) drafting traps, this week's work, Mile Marker #9, and an in-class discussion. Every slide has a talk track in the speaker notes, so the deck also works as a script for an online mini-lecture. Pair it with the STAR(T) Stories overview deck.

Coaching notes

Where students tend to get stuck this module, and what helps.

  • Re-entry after spring break is real. Some students don't come back on schedule. A short, warm check-in during the first few days back catches students before Module 9 becomes two missing modules.
  • Question 1 needs a real example. Naming a thinking trap is easy; showing when it happened is the point. Look for a specific moment and a better question the student could ask next time.
  • The first STAR(T) draft is supposed to be rough. Question 2 is a first draft. Encourage honesty over polish, and point to the most common gaps: "we" instead of "I," and a Transfer that says "this will help me in the future."
  • Question 3 should show a change. The 4C Check is only useful if it changes something. Look for what the student added, cut, or corrected after rereading.
  • This draft can become a capstone story. Tell students to hold onto it. Many will reuse it as one of their four STAR(T) Stories, which makes Modules 11 and 12 lighter.
  • Privacy in stories. Stories often involve coworkers, family, or classmates. Students don't need to use other people's names.

Active learning ideas

Pick one or two. Each fits in a 50-minute class alongside the slides.

4C Check, live

Time: 10 minutesYou need: A real, low-stakes decision someone in the room is facing

  1. Ask for a volunteer decision, or offer one of your own.
  2. Walk it through Clarity, Context, Credibility, and Consequences, writing answers on the board.
  3. Ask: did the decision change, or just get clearer?

Why it works: Students see the tool work before they use it in the AI Chat and Mile Marker.

Online sections: Walk one decision through the four Cs in your recorded mini-lecture.

Trap spotting

Time: 10 minutesYou need: Five short statements on the screen, one per thought trap

  1. Show statements like "I failed one quiz, so I'm bad at math."
  2. Groups name the trap and rewrite the thought.
  3. Students write down the trap that feels most like them, for Mile Marker #9, Question 1.

Why it works: Gives students language for Question 1 and makes the traps easy to recognize.

Online sections: Post the statements as a quick self-check in an announcement.

Say it in two minutes

Time: 10 minutesYou need: Students' STAR(T) drafts, and a timer

  1. Partners take turns telling their draft out loud in two minutes.
  2. The listener names the weakest step.
  3. Each student makes one fix before submitting.

Why it works: Early practice saying a story out loud makes the capstone recordings much easier.

Online sections: Encourage students to read their draft out loud once before submitting.

In-class discussion

What are you really deciding?

  1. Think of a decision you're facing right now. What are you really deciding?
  2. What's one thing you know, and one thing you're assuming?

Talk with a partner for 3 minutes, then share what changed.

Where it comes from: Builds toward the AI Chat: Think It Through. (Module 9 has no online discussion.)

Facilitating: Model it with a low-stakes decision of your own. Ask what changed when pairs separated what they know from what they're assuming; that's Credibility, and it's usually the turning point.
