Module 11: Making an Impact — Community, Values & Your Pathfinder Journey

Slides, coaching notes, active learning ideas, and an in-class discussion.

Slides, coaching notes, and in-class ideas for Module 11: Making an Impact — Community, Values & Your Pathfinder Journey.

At a glance

  • I can define community broadly and identify at least one community I already belong to and contribute to.
  • I can describe specific ways my everyday actions make a positive impact on the people around me.
  • I can connect my strengths and values to one intentional way I want to grow my community impact going forward.
  • I can select four experiences from my semester and match each one to an Essential Skill for my STAR(T) Story.

Main assignment Mile Marker #11: My Community Impact

Slides

Title, a Breaking Camp housekeeping slide for your announcements, the Skill Spotlight, community as a system, what you offer and what you're not available for, the Start Your STAR(T) Stories Trail Tip, this week's work, Mile Marker #11, and an in-class discussion. Every slide has a talk track in the speaker notes, so the deck also works as a script for an online mini-lecture. Pair it with the STAR(T) Stories overview deck.

Coaching notes

Where students tend to get stuck this module, and what helps.

  • "Civic engagement" can feel like it's for other people. Students who don't vote or volunteer sometimes assume this module isn't for them. Start with communities they're already in: work, family, a team, a class. Small and real is exactly right.
  • Question 2 should use the system. The strongest answers name the people, rules, or influence in a community and point to one specific change. Generic "I help people" answers usually skipped the Community Snapshot.
  • Question 4 is the capstone checkpoint. Part II asks students to name the story for each of their four STAR(T) skills. It's your best early warning: students who leave it blank are at risk of not finishing the capstone. Follow up this week.
  • The journal surfaces boundaries. Getting the Call asks students how they'd answer a request on their own terms. Some students write about being overcommitted. Affirm a clearly stated limit as a skill, not a failure.
  • Devices matter for gathering stories. Guidebook forms save only in the browser on the device a student used. Students who switched devices will find their submitted Mile Markers in Blackboard.
  • STAR(T) Stories are due Sunday. Remind students now that the capstone and Module 12 are due Sunday, not Monday, so instructors can grade before final grades are due.

Active learning ideas

Pick one or two. Each fits in a 50-minute class alongside the slides.

The shared kitchen

Time: 10 minutesYou need: The worked example from Lesson 11.2

  1. Read the scenario: four people share a kitchen, the dishes pile up, nobody says anything.
  2. Groups name the people, rules, influence, and one leverage point.
  3. Each student applies the same four parts to one of their own communities.

Why it works: Practices the systems lens on something everyone recognizes before students apply it to Mile Marker #11.

Online sections: Walk the example through in your recorded mini-lecture.

Four skills, four stories

Time: 15 minutesYou need: Students' Mile Markers, in Blackboard or saved PDFs

  1. Students skim their Mile Markers for moments that show each of their four skills.
  2. They write one phrase per skill for Mile Marker #11, Question 4.
  3. Partners check: is each story real, specific, and theirs?

Why it works: Turns Question 4 into working time and gets every student to a plan for the capstone.

Online sections: Point students to the Find Your Stories prompts in the STAR(T) Stories Guide.

Say one story

Time: 10 minutesYou need: A timer

  1. Each student tells one story to a partner in two minutes.
  2. The listener names the thinnest step.
  3. Swap.

Why it works: Saying a story out loud once makes recording it much easier.

Online sections: Encourage students to say one story out loud to anyone before they record.

In-class discussion

Getting the call

  1. A group connected to something you care about asks for your help. What do you say yes to?
  2. What's one thing you're not available for, and how would you say so kindly?

Talk with a partner for 3 minutes, then share one way to say no well.

Where it comes from: An in-class version of the private online journal: Getting the Call.

Facilitating: Keep it hypothetical and low-stakes. Listen for limits stated kindly and clearly, and name that as a skill: saying no well is part of showing up well.
