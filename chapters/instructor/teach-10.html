Module 10: Team Up — Collaboration & People Skills

Slides, coaching notes, active learning ideas, and an in-class discussion.

16 of 20 in this guide

Slides, coaching notes, and in-class ideas for Module 10: Team Up — Collaboration & People Skills.

At a glance

  • I can identify my natural collaboration tendencies using my 16Personalities results and explain how they show up in group work.
  • I can name two collaboration strengths and one or two challenges and explain why those challenges happen.
  • I can describe three specific strategies that help me work more effectively with others.
  • I can articulate in one clear sentence what I need from teammates to do my best work.

Slides

Coaching notes

Where students tend to get stuck this module, and what helps.

  • Group work stories come with feelings. Many students carry a bad group project story. Let them tell it, but keep it to roles, not names, and steer toward what would have helped.
  • Question 1: own words, not the official description. Students sometimes paste the 16Personalities description. The question asks for one short phrase in their own words, plus strengths and challenges that honestly fit.
  • Question 2 is a tendency, not a flaw. Students explain why one challenge happens. Some will mention neurodivergence; sharing it is always optional. Respond to the insight, not the label.
  • Question 3 should be one sentence. "I need teammates to tell me early if plans change" is the target: simple, respectful, and specific.
  • Merit #3 is the last one. Students who are behind on Merit run out of time here. Check who still needs an activity early in the week, and point online students to a virtual meet-up.
  • 16Personalities isn't a diagnosis. Treat the type as a map, not a rulebook. If a student feels boxed in by their result, that's worth naming in feedback.

Active learning ideas

Pick one or two. Each fits in a 50-minute class alongside the slides.

Online sections · Merit #3 is due

Host a virtual meet-up

Time: 30 minutesYou need: A Teams meeting created inside your course

  1. Welcome and introductions, about 5 minutes.
  2. One topic, about 15 minutes. This module: What this semester has taught you so far
  3. Open questions, about 10 minutes.

Why it works: It warms students up for the STAR(T) Stories in Modules 11 and 12. A meet-up you host counts as a Merit activity, and it's the one option that works from anywhere and still puts online students with other people.

Remind students to screenshot the meeting window for their Merit proof. Full guidance: Virtual meet-ups.

Role corners

Time: 10 minutesYou need: The seven group roles from Lesson 10.2 posted around the room

  1. Students stand by the role that sounds most like them.
  2. Each group names one thing their role brings and one way it frustrates other roles.
  3. Students move to their second role and compare.

Why it works: Makes group differences visible and normal before students write about their own.

Online sections: Ask students to name their top two roles in the discussion.

Set the expectations

Time: 10 minutesYou need: A sample group assignment

  1. Groups answer the four questions from Lesson 10.5: the task, the deadlines, who's doing what, and what done looks like.
  2. Each group writes one group norm.
  3. Debrief: which question would have saved their worst group project?

Why it works: Practices the strategy that prevents most group conflict.

Online sections: Share the four questions in your announcement as a template for any group project.

AI group plan, checked

Time: 15 minutesYou need: A sample group assignment and an AI tool, if the assignment rules allow it

  1. Generate a task list and timeline with AI using the Trail Tip's steps.
  2. Groups mark what AI got wrong or couldn't know, like who works evenings.
  3. Adjust the plan together.

Why it works: Shows both the usefulness and the limits of AI for planning, and reinforces BE Responsible.

Online sections: Demonstrate the Trail Tip in your recorded mini-lecture.

In-class discussion

When group work went sideways

  1. Tell one group work story: good, bad, or somewhere in between. What went sideways?
  2. What would have helped? Does your 16Personalities type explain any of it?

No names. Talk with a partner for 3 minutes, then share what would have helped.

Where it comes from: An in-class version of the online discussion: That One Time Group Work Went Sideways. In-person students bring a story and their type.

Facilitating: Set the no-names ground rule first. Collect what would have helped; most answers map to Lesson 10.5, especially clear expectations.
