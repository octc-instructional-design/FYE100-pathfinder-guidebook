Module 1: Your Pathfinder Journey Begins

Slides, coaching notes, active learning ideas, and an in-class discussion.

7 of 20 in this guide

Slides, coaching notes, and in-class ideas for Module 1: Your Pathfinder Journey Begins.

At a glance

  • I can describe my personal reasons for attending college and define what success means to me right now.
  • I can navigate Blackboard and locate key course information, assignments, and instructor contact details.
  • I can set one personal or academic goal for this semester and describe how I plan to reach it.
  • I can explain what artificial intelligence is and identify where it already shows up in my daily life.

Slides

Title, a Base Camp welcome slide for your announcements, one or two slides per lesson, the Office 365 Trail Tip, this week's work, Mile Marker #1, and an in-class discussion. Every slide has a talk track in the speaker notes, so the deck also works as a script for an online mini-lecture.

Coaching notes

Where students tend to get stuck this module, and what helps.

  • Two very different starting points share the room. Recent high school graduates (including dual-credit students as young as 16) and adults returning after years away often sit side by side. Lesson 1.1 speaks to both on purpose. Naming both out loud on day one tells returning adults their work experience counts, and tells younger students the support is there if they go looking.
  • "I didn't know" usually means "I didn't read the syllabus." Expect questions this week that the syllabus already answers. Point students back to it kindly and specifically ("It's under Late Work on page 3"). That's the habit Lesson 1.1 is building.
  • Mile Marker #1, Question 4: goals that are too vague. "Do better" and "pass" are the common weak goals. Use the book's test: could someone else tell whether you reached it, and could you put each step on a calendar? Students reuse this goal later in the course, so it's worth a quick nudge now.
  • Question 5: every stance on AI is a fair answer. Some students are excited about AI, some are skeptical, and some would rather not use it at all. The question asks for a clear position, not a positive one. Make that explicit, especially for students who expect the course to push AI.
  • The BE Check is practice for the vocabulary. Students match the five BE behaviors from Lesson 1.3 to real situations. If several students miss the same item, clear up that behavior in your next announcement or class. The same five behaviors come back all semester.
  • Blackboard basics trip up more students than the content. Finding the module, submitting a file, and reading feedback are new to many students. The optional Syllabus and Blackboard Scavenger Hunt is low-stakes practice; encourage anyone who seems lost to try it before Mile Marker #1 is due.
  • Ask for profile photos early. A photo in Blackboard (Lesson 1.5) makes discussions and feedback feel personal for everyone, you included. A short reminder in week 1 gets most students to do it.

Active learning ideas

Pick one or two. Each fits in a 50-minute class alongside the slides.

Before and now

Time: 10 minutesYou need: The four Before / Now shifts from Lesson 1.1, on the screen

  1. Students silently pick the shift that will take the most adjustment for them.
  2. They move to a corner of the room assigned to that shift (or raise a hand for each one).
  3. Each group names one thing they'll do this week to get ahead of it, and shares it with the room.

Why it works: Turns Lesson 1.1's reflection question into a visible, low-risk conversation, and students see they aren't the only ones.

Online sections: Post the four shifts as a poll or quick survey, and share the results in your next announcement.

Syllabus speed hunt

Time: 10 minutesYou need: The FYE 100 syllabus (or another course's syllabus) open on devices

  1. In pairs, students race to find five things: a due date, the late work policy, how to contact you, the AI Course Policy, and one thing that surprised them.
  2. First pair with all five shares where they found each one.
  3. Close by asking which item they'd have missed if they'd only skimmed.

Why it works: Practices the "syllabus is a contract" habit with the real document, not a description of it.

Online sections: Point students to the optional Syllabus and Blackboard Scavenger Hunt in Blackboard, which does the same job.

Name that tune

Time: 5 minutesYou need: Your voice

  1. Hum the bassline from Lesson 1.3 and ask the room what song it is.
  2. Let both answers come out, then ask: was anyone wrong?
  3. Connect it to AI: it completes the most likely pattern, which isn't always the right answer. Introduce the word hallucination.

Why it works: A memorable, low-tech way into pattern completion. Students remember the song long after they forget a definition.

Online sections: Tell the story in your recorded mini-lecture; it works just as well on video.

In-class discussion

Who's on the trail with you?

  1. Your name, and what you're here for: a program, a goal, or a fresh start.
  2. One thing you're bringing with you: a skill, a habit, or a story.

Pair up for 3 minutes, then introduce your partner to the room.

Where it comes from: An in-class version of the online Trailhead Introductions discussion. Introducing a partner, instead of yourself, lowers the stakes on day one.

Facilitating: Model it first with your own answer. Listen for strengths students name in passing (work ethic, caring for family, a trade skill) and point them out: those are the answers to Mile Marker #1, Question 3.
