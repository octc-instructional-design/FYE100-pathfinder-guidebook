Question 1Your Type, Strengths & Challenges

Example: "INFJ-T: quiet planner who likes meaningful work. I'm an organized planner and a calm mediator, but I tend to avoid conflict and take on too much." Strengths word bank: clear communicator · supportive teammate · creative problem-solver · organized planner · calm mediator · detail-checker · energizer or motivator · deep thinker · flexible adapter · relationship-builder · big-picture visionary · dependable finisher. Challenges word bank: hard to get started · taking on too much · hard to speak up · avoiding conflict · coming across too blunt · overwhelmed by chaos · frustrated by unclear instructions · perfectionism · withdrawing under stress · trouble reading tone · over-controlling · resisting schedules.

A strong answer

Gives your type in your own words, and names two strengths and one or two challenges that sound like you, not like a quiz summary.

Look back at: Lesson 10.3: Your letters; Lesson 10.4: Strengths and struggles

Question 2Why, and What Helps

Think of your challenge as a tendency, not a flaw: needing structure, being introverted, processing fast or slow, group anxiety, or how your brain works. Sharing anything about neurodivergence is always optional. Strategies word bank: start with clear expectations · communicate my style · play to my strengths · ask clarifying questions · break tasks into pieces · use tools wisely · assume good intent · know my stress signals · use the 2-minute reset.

A strong answer

Explains the why behind one challenge, names three strategies that fit you, and connects one to a real experience.

Look back at: Lesson 10.4: Different brains; Lesson 10.5: Nine strategies

Question 3What You Need From Your Team

Examples: "I work best when tasks and deadlines are clearly divided." "I thrive when I can think before speaking."

A strong answer

Is one clear sentence that's specific, respectful, and something you could actually say to a real team.
